What a book costs

Cost of goods

A playthrough is two kinds of call. The outline fires once on Opus and is the single most expensive request in the product — roughly 3,000 tokens of nested JSON out, which is also why a truncated outline is the failure the README warns about. The turns are cheap and flat: the engine assembles about 2,800 tokens of context per turn regardless of how long the run gets, so cost scales with turn count and nothing else.

A lore book changes both halves. The document goes to the outline whole, up to 24,000 characters marked canon, which is the largest single block of input the product ever sends. Then two ~900-character passages ride along with every turn — flat by design, so a hundred-page document costs no more per turn than a one-page one. Lines marked † apply only to runs with a lore book loaded.

At the balanced mix a book costs about $0.39 to serve. The outline is 21% of that and it is fixed — a player who quits after three turns still costs you the full outline.

What to charge

Four structures

The danger in this product is a one-time purchase against an open-ended serving cost: a reader who buys once for $15 and plays forty books has cost you more than they paid. Every structure below either meters the books or caps them.

The number to quote

Recommendation

Retail at $1 a book and $12 a month for twenty. A dollar is a legible price for a thing that takes an evening, it sits at a comfortable multiple of what the book costs to serve, and the monthly figure lands where a paperback gamebook lands.

Lead with the free first book. It costs you one outline and a run of turns to find out whether a stranger likes the product, which is cheaper than any ad you could buy — but gate it behind an email or a device check, because unmetered it is a faucet.

Keep the bring-your-own-key build alive alongside the hosted tier. It has no cost of goods at all, it absorbs exactly the heavy users who would otherwise erode the subscription, and it is the version that already runs on this machine.

What moves the number

Levers & exposure
  • Open relay The proxy has no auth. serve.py binds 127.0.0.1, which is the only thing protecting the key today. The moment it is hosted, /api/sample will accept any request that reaches it and bill you for the answer. Auth and a per-account rate limit are a cost control, not a feature.
  • Outline retries The outline is the priciest call and the one that fails. Every retry is a fresh $0.08. Play anyway is the cheap path — it also happens to be the cheapest recovery on the sheet.
  • The lore book The one input a customer controls that changes what you pay. Up to 24,000 characters go to the outline whole as canon — about 6,000 tokens of Opus input, or $0.03 on top of every outline. Retrieval during play is the cheap half and deliberately flat: two ~900-character passages a turn whether the document is one page or a hundred. All in, $0.05 a run. The 24,000-character truncation is already acting as a cost control; that is worth knowing before anyone raises it.
  • Tier default Brisk versus Considered is the largest single dial you own: $0.24 against $0.40 a book. Defaulting new readers to Brisk and offering Considered as the deliberate choice moves blended cost more than any prompt edit will.
  • Prompt caching The narrator rules are identical across every turn of a run. Cached, that prefix reads at a tenth of its cost. Worth perhaps 6% of turn spend here — real, but check cache_read_input_tokens is non-zero, since a short prefix may fall under the minimum cacheable length and silently never cache.
  • The ceiling holds MARGIN_MAX_TOKENS at 4,096 is a hard cap on the worst case of any one call. Raising it to rescue outlines raises your maximum exposure per request in the same motion.
  • Heavy tail At 20 books a month a subscriber costs $7.84 and still clears. Without a cap there is no figure to put here at all — which is the whole argument for the cap.
Rates: Haiku 4.5 $1/$5, Sonnet 5 $2/$10, Opus 5 $5/$25 per million tokens in/out. Processing at 2.9% + $0.30 per charge. Infrastructure modelled at $18/mo plus $0.02 per subscriber.
